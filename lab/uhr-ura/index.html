Why this is tricky

English counts from the hour that has passed: half past two. German and Slovenian count toward the coming hour: halb drei, pol treh, literally half (of the) three. In Austria the quarters work the same way, viertel drei and dreiviertel drei, matching Slovenian četrt na tri and tri četrt na tri, while standard German says Viertel nach zwei and Viertel vor drei. Kids growing up between two of these systems meet all of them at once.

How the game teaches

Four short levels, each adding one idea: full hours, halves, quarters, five-minute steps. Read tasks and set tasks alternate, so a child both decodes and produces. Wrong answers are cheap and visible: pick the wrong sentence and its clock appears as red ghost hands next to the real ones; set the wrong time and the game reads your clock back to you. The coloured slice only appears as a hint after two tries. Every level ends with a why question to talk about together before revealing an answer.

Speech uses the voices already installed in your browser, so how it sounds (and whether there is a Slovenian voice) depends on your device. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere.
