Why two null points, and why these ones

Tracking error is the angle between the cartridge's cantilever and the groove's tangent. It never disappears on a pivoted arm: push it to zero at one radius and it grows at another. Two things are yours to choose — how far the stylus overshoots the spindle (overhang) and how far the cartridge is twisted in the headshell (offset angle) — and those two choices buy exactly two zeros, the null radii.

The audible cost is second-harmonic distortion, and it grows with the error angle but also with how slowly the groove is passing under the stylus. That is why the graph can be weighted by radius: the same one degree of error does far more damage near the label, where the groove crawls past at 21 cm/s, than at the rim, where it races by at 51 cm/s.

Löfgren A, which most people know as Baerwald, spreads that weighted error into three equal humps, so the worst moment is as small as it can be. Löfgren B trades bigger errors at the two ends for a lower average across the side. Stevenson pins one null right at the inner groove, keeping the last track cleanest at the cost of everything before it — a reasonable bet for classical sides that end loud, and the alignment many Japanese headshells assume.

None of this fixes the arc. A linear-tracking arm has no tracking error at all; it just has every other problem instead.

The maths, and how honest these numbers are

With spindle, pivot and stylus forming a triangle, the error at groove radius r is ε(r) = asin((r² + L² − D²) / (2 L r)) − β, where L is effective length, D the pivot-to-spindle distance and β the offset angle. Zeros at n₁ and n₂ require sin β = (n₁+n₂)/2L and D = √(L² − n₁n₂), which is why overhang and offset always come as a pair.

Löfgren's trick was to substitute u = 1/r, which turns the weighted error into a plain quadratic in u. The equal-ripple answer is then a Chebyshev polynomial (that is Löfgren A) and the lowest-average answer is a least-squares fit (Löfgren B). This page solves both directly, so the numbers move with whatever inner and outer radius you choose rather than coming from a lookup table.

For IEC radii that puts Löfgren A at 66.0 and 120.9 mm and Stevenson at 60.3 and 117.4 mm, matching the published figures to a tenth of a millimetre. The Löfgren B nulls here land a few tenths below the 70.3/116.6 that most protractors print — those older figures come from Löfgren's approximate formulas, and a 2021 re-derivation in the AES Journal found the circulating values off by up to 0.6 mm in just this direction. For setting up a cartridge the difference is smaller than your eyesight.
