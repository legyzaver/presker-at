How it works

Every layer is compared only to the one right below it. If a layer's edge sticks out past the edge of the layer below by more than the layer height times the tangent of the overhang angle, that step is steeper than the limit — the plastic would be extruded into thin air and either droops or fails. Lower the limit and more layers get flagged; raise it (real printers vary — cheap ones are stricter, around 45°) and some marginal overhangs are allowed through.

A bridge is different: it's open air with solid material on both sides, not hanging off one edge. A taut string of plastic can span a short gap like that on its own — here, up to 30mm — without anything underneath. The bridge shape's middle span is right at that limit on purpose.

Turning on supports doesn't change the shape, only whether the flagged layers get a scaffold underneath them: the flags clear, but it costs extra filament and print time, which is exactly the trade a real slicer is making for you. Time and filament here are rough, relative estimates — not your printer's real numbers, just enough to compare settings against each other.
