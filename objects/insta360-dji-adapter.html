Insta360 One RS 1-Inch → DJI Osmo Pocket 3 Battery Adapter

Insta360 stopped making the battery for the One RS 1-Inch 360 (the Leica edition). It's a superb camera that a lot of people now can't power. This printed adapter runs the camera core off a DJI Osmo Pocket 3 battery over USB-C — so the camera lives again.

↓ Download the model on Thingiverse

The problem

The Insta360 One RS 1-Inch 360 Edition — co-engineered with Leica — is one of the best consumer 360 cameras ever made: two 1-inch sensors, Super-Summicron lenses, genuinely cinematic footage. But it takes a specific battery base, and Insta360 no longer produces it. If yours dies, or you want a spare, there's nothing to buy. A lot of people are sitting on an expensive camera they can't switch on.

The idea

The camera core also has a USB-C port, and the One RS will run on USB-C power. Meanwhile the DJI Osmo Pocket 3 battery handle is still in production, cheap, widely available — and almost exactly the right size and shape to stand in for the missing Insta360 base. So: use the DJI battery as the power source, and print an adapter that holds the two together and routes power from one USB-C port to the other.

How it works

The printed adapter is a shell that mates the camera core to the DJI battery, in the same footprint the original base occupied. Inside it sits a short USB-C extender that carries power from the DJI battery's USB-C output up into the camera's USB-C input. No soldering, no electronics to build — the extender does the electrical work and the print does the mechanical work of holding everything rigid and aligned.

Assembled

Stack it up — DJI battery, printed adapter, camera core — and the One RS powers on and records, exactly as it did on its original base.

What you need

  • Your Insta360 One RS 1-Inch 360 core — the part you already have and can't power.
  • A DJI Osmo Pocket 3 battery handle — the power source.
  • A USB-C extender adapter — I used the Basesailor USB-C Extender (PD 100W, 40Gbps, 3-pack). Any short, full-spec USB-C extender of the same size should work.
  • The printed adapter — free on Thingiverse.

Honesty

This is a power adapter, not a reverse-engineered battery: it feeds the camera over USB-C rather than through the original contact base. It's a workaround born of necessity — if you print it, check the fit of your own USB-C extender and camera, and use it with sense. It rescued mine; I'm sharing it so it can rescue yours.
